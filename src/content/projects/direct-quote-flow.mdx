  - component: ProjectIntro
    props:
      eyebrow: Perfect Pet & Now Pet Insurance — Case study 04
      title: Direct Quote Flow Redesign
      summary: >-
        The direct quote flow is the journey customers begin when they select "Get a quote" on the
        Perfect Pet or Now Pet website. Because they arrive directly from the brand site, the flow needs
        to read as a continuation of it, and because they are being asked for information before they
        have been offered anything in return, the form itself should demand as little attention as
        possible. When a form is designed well, customers move from one question to the next without
        stopping to consider how long it will take or whether the effort is worthwhile. The existing
        flow did the opposite. It presented every question on a single page, asked for the pet's full
        health and behaviour history before showing a price, and lost approximately one in five users
        at each of its five steps.


        Only a small number of answers are used to calculate a price: the pet's species, breed, age,
        sex, purchase cost and neutering status, together with the owner's date of birth and postcode.
        The redesign is organised around reaching that price as quickly as possible. The flow now
        begins with the pet's basic details, followed by the owner's email address, date of birth,
        address and preferred start date, and the questions that determine which type of cover is
        shown. The quotes follow immediately. The health questions, warranty checks and remaining
        personal details are asked afterwards, once the customer has seen a price and has a reason to
        continue. In the process, I removed a question on which the price did not depend, combined three behaviour and insurance-history questions into a single checklist asked
        once per household, and ended the repetition of every health question for each additional pet.
        The result is a shorter route to the quote and a form that asks its more demanding questions at
        a point where customers are prepared to answer them.

  - component: ProjectFindingsGrid
    props:
      title: Findings from the audits, contact analysis and heuristic workshop
      items:
        - title: Questions asked before they were needed
          body: >-
            The price depends on a short list of answers, yet the flow asked for considerably more
            before showing one. Health and behaviour questions become relevant only once a customer has
            decided to buy, and asking them first made the form feel like a far greater commitment
            than was necessary.
        - title: Treatment of multi-pet households
          body: >-
            The flow assumed a single pet. The multi-pet discount appeared only on the quotes page, as
            a strikethrough price without explanation, and some customers who wished to insure several
            pets were directed to telephone for a quote. Multi-pet quotes and policies account for a
            significant share of the direct flow, so this required proper consideration in the design.
        - title: Uncertainty about pre-existing conditions
          body: >-
            This was the most frequent reason for customers pausing or calling. The online question
            accepts only a yes or no answer, whereas most veterinary histories are less clear-cut, and
            the accompanying note conflated pre-existing conditions with the [[waiting period]].
        - title: Late discovery of eligibility
          body: >-
            Some customers completed most of a quote before learning that their address or their dog's
            breed could not be covered.
        - title: Cover questions framed in insurance terms
          body: >-
            The [[demands and needs]] questions did not correspond to decisions a pet owner would
            recognise, and because their only effect was to change the order in which quotes were
            displayed, colleagues questioned their purpose.
        - title: Lack of orientation
          body: >-
            With every question on one page and a progress bar that named only the five pages,
            customers had no indication of how close they were to a price. This concern gave the first
            workshop station its name.

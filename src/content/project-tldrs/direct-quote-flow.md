**Problem:** The direct quote flow is the journey customers begin when they select "Get a quote" on the brand website. It asked for most of its questions, including a full set of health and behaviour questions for each pet, before showing a price, and approximately one in five users left at each step. Customers with two pets were required to answer every question twice.

**Approach:** I audited the flow against Nielsen's heuristics and the Baymard checklist, analysed customer contacts and call recordings, and ran a heuristic workshop and a co-design workshop with colleagues from across the business. I tested individual ideas with Lyssna. I designed in Figma and used Claude, configured with our design tokens, to build interactive demonstrations for stakeholders and the board.

**Constraints:** Some answers are used to calculate the price and others support underwriting or regulatory requirements, so every change to the question set was agreed with pricing and underwriting. The flow serves two brands on shared infrastructure, and Perfect Pet introduced a new visual identity during the project.

**Outcome:** The flow now asks for the pet's basic details, the owner's email address, date of birth and address, and the cover preferences, then shows the quotes, and asks the health questions, warranty checks and remaining personal details afterwards. The pet colour question has been removed, three behaviour and insurance-history questions have been combined into one checklist completed once per household, and multi-pet households no longer repeat every question. Questions are presented one at a time under a named progress indicator, and the revised flow is scheduled for the next sprint.

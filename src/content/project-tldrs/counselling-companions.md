**Problem:** Most counsellors and therapists work alone or in small practices, without the IT and admin support a bigger organisation has. They get by with email, WhatsApp, video calls, a calendar and a spreadsheet, none of which were built to work together, so a client's details end up in five places. One practitioner told me that for every 40-minute session, 15 to 20 minutes goes on admin.

**Approach:** For my MSc dissertation I interviewed seven practitioners and coded the interviews with reflexive thematic analysis. I then ran a week-long co-design workshop by post and email, because they didn't have time for anything live. That led to a Figma prototype of a practice dashboard with a voice assistant, which I tested with a heuristic evaluation, a think-aloud session, and a Responsible Research and Innovation review.

**Constraints:** This is a sensitive setting and practitioners have strong, often opposite views on AI. I kept the design to the back office, away from the therapy itself, and treated careless AI use as a real ethical risk.

**Outcome:** A tested concept and prototype. The AI is switched on per client, not for the whole practice. It explains what it's about to do and waits for the practitioner to approve it before anything touches a client record. Both of those came out of the ethics review, and they were what people responded to most. It's a small sample and not a shipped product; the next step would be an MVP on real Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 data.
